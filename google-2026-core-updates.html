Google's 2026 core updates: who won and who lost

By John Masajo · Published June 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Google rolled out two core updates in the first half of 2026, one in March and one in May. Looking at both, the pattern is clear enough to plan around. The good news is it rewards the same things a good SEO strategy should already be going after.

What a core update is

A core update isn't a penalty. It's Google re-checking the quality of everything in its index and changing how it weighs what's helpful and trustworthy. Sites don't usually drop for breaking one rule. They get re-ranked against an updated idea of what deserves to rank. That's why recovering is rarely about one quick fix.

Who won

Brands and original sources. Sites with their own data, first-hand experience, and real depth. Sites people search for by name. In both updates, the sites that went up were the ones adding something new to the web, not repackaging what's already out there. Google's own messaging kept saying the same thing: originality and authority are what it's rewarding.

Who lost

Aggregators and sites that mostly collect or summarize other people's work. Thin, copied content. Pages that put a new layer on top of existing content without adding anything. It makes sense: now that AI can summarize a topic on its own, sites that only summarize have lost their reason to rank.

The main takeaway

Original, authoritative content beats volume and rehashed content, again. This isn't new, but every core update makes it clearer. The sites that keep winning are the ones the rest of the web treats as a source, and that's earned on other sites as much as on your own. Core updates keep rewarding brands people talk about and link to. So the real fix isn't a technical scramble. It's the slow work of becoming a source worth citing.

If an update hit your site

Don't go looking for a quick fix. Core updates reward lasting quality, and there's no switch to flip. You recover by actually getting better (more original, more authoritative, more useful) by the time the next update comes. It's slow, but it's the only thing that lasts.

The 2026 core updates said what Google has been saying for years, just louder: be the original source, not the site that repackages it. Build for that, and updates stop being something you worry about.
