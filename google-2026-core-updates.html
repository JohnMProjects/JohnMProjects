Google's 2026 core updates: who won and who lost

By John Masajo · Published June 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Google ran two broad core updates in the first half of 2026, one in March and one in May. Looking at both together, the pattern is consistent enough to plan around, and the good news is that it rewards the same thing a sensible strategy should already be chasing.

What a core update is, quickly

A core update is not a penalty. It is a broad reassessment of quality across the whole index, a re-weighting of what Google considers helpful and trustworthy. Sites do not get punished for a specific rule so much as re-sorted against a refreshed sense of what deserves to rank. That is why recovery is rarely about one quick fix.

Who won

Brands and primary sources. Sites with original data, first-hand experience, and real depth. The destinations people actively seek out by name. Across both updates, the visible movement favored places that add something to the web rather than repackage it, and Google's own messaging kept reinforcing that originality and authority are what it is rewarding.

Who lost

Aggregators and sites whose main function is to compile or summarize other people's work. Thin, derivative content. Pages that added a layer of wrapping but no new value. There is a logic to it: as AI got better at summarizing on its own, the human summarizers lost the thing that justified their ranking. If a model can rehash a topic instantly, a site that only rehashes has no edge left.

The through-line

Originality and authority beat volume and rehashing, again. This is not a new lesson, but each core update sharpens it and raises the floor. The sites that keep winning are the ones the rest of the web treats as sources, and that treatment is earned as much off-page as on. Core updates keep rewarding the brands people talk about and link to, which means the durable response is not a technical scramble but the slow work of becoming a source worth citing.

If an update hit you

Resist the urge to chase a quick fix. Core updates reward durable quality, and there is no toggle to flip. Recovery comes from genuinely being better, more original, more authoritative, more useful, by the time the next assessment rolls around. Painful, but honest, and it is the only thing that lasts.

The 2026 core updates said the familiar thing a little louder: be the original, authoritative source, not the site that repackages one. Build for that, and updates stop being events you brace for and start being ones you quietly benefit from.
