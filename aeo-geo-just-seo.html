AEO and GEO explained: a new discipline, or just SEO with a new name?

By John Masajo · Published May 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Two acronyms took over SEO conversations this year. AEO, Answer Engine Optimization, and GEO, Generative Engine Optimization. The pitch behind both is that AI search is so different you need a brand new discipline to compete in it, and, conveniently, a brand new set of tools to pay for. Google's own words tell a calmer story.

What Google actually said

In May 2026, Google published its first consolidated guide to optimizing for generative AI features in Search, covering AI Overviews and AI Mode. The central message was blunt: there is no separate strategy for AI search. The fundamentals that earn ordinary rankings, unique content, genuine expertise, clear structure, are the same things that earn a citation inside an AI answer. Google also reaffirmed that classic SEO is the foundation AI visibility is built on, not a thing it replaces.

So what do AEO and GEO mean?

Stripped of the marketing, they describe the same goal from two angles. AEO is about being the answer an engine pulls when someone asks a direct question. GEO is about being the source a generative engine quotes or synthesizes when it writes a response. Both come down to one thing: being the source a machine trusts enough to hand to a user.

The part that is genuinely new

There is a real shift, and it is worth naming honestly. The win condition changed. For twenty years the goal was to rank number one and earn the click. Now a large share of queries end inside an AI answer, so the goal is increasingly to be the cited source inside that answer. That rewards a few specific things: claims a model can extract cleanly, content structured so the relevant passage is easy to lift, clarity about who you are as an entity, and being named on trusted third-party sites. None of that is exotic. It is E-E-A-T and technical clarity, pointed at a new surface.

The part that is just hype

A few popular tactics are noise, and some carry downside. You do not need to chop your content into tiny AI-bait chunks; Google's own team advised against it and confirmed its systems parse full pages and extract passages themselves. You do not need to abandon what already works. And seeding fake mentions to look authoritative is a spam risk, not a strategy. The brands winning AI visibility are the ones doing the fundamentals unusually well, not the ones chasing a new acronym.

My take, from the off-page side

The half of this that most GEO advice skips is the one I work in. AI engines trust sources that are talked about and linked to across the open web. Authority earned off your own site is a big part of what gets you retrieved and quoted. So the version of GEO I actually run looks a lot like good digital PR and clean, relevant link building, plus content clear enough for a machine to quote. Learn the vocabulary, skip the panic. Do the fundamentals well and you are already doing both.

Sources: Google Search Central, May 2026 guide to optimizing for generative AI features; Google I/O 2026 Search announcements.
