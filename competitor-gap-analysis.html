Competitor gap analysis: aim before you fire

By John Masajo · Published March 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Most link building starts with a verb: "let's get links." Mine starts with a question. What is already working for the sites beating us, and can I earn the same, then add more? That question is what a gap analysis answers, and it is the difference between spraying outreach and aiming it.

What a gap analysis actually is

It is a comparison. You put your backlink and keyword profile next to your real competitors and look for two things. First, the referring domains that link to several of them but not to you, the backlink intersect. Second, the keywords they rank for that you do not. Those two gaps are your shortlist for the whole campaign, one for outreach, one for content.

Why it has to come first

Because it changes the odds before you spend any effort. A site that already links to three of your competitors is far more likely to link to you than a cold prospect who has never linked in your space. It has shown, in public, that it links to businesses like yours. Aiming outreach at proven linkers lifts your reply and placement rate and cuts the wasted hours that make link building feel like a slog. You are not convincing someone to start linking. You are giving someone who already does a reason to include you too.

How I run it, tool agnostic

The tools differ, the logic does not. This works in Ahrefs, Semrush, or anything with an intersect report.

1. Pick three to five true competitors. Not the biggest brands in the space, the sites actually ranking for your money terms. Those are the ones whose link profile is relevant to yours.

2. Run the backlink intersect. Pull the domains linking to two or more competitors but not to you. That list is the core of your prospecting, already filtered for relevance by the market itself.

3. Vet before you value it. Relevance first, then real authority against a sensible Domain Rating floor, then a human check that the site is genuinely editorial and not a link farm wearing a nice logo. A big number on a junk domain is still junk.

4. Run the keyword gap. Surface the terms competitors rank for that you do not, and keep the winnable ones. Those become your content targets: the linkable assets worth building, or the pages your new links should point at.

5. Prioritize, then reach out. Rank the list by relevance and winnability, not raw volume, and start outreach at the top. Every pitch now has a real reason attached to it.

What it does and does not promise

Gap analysis tells you where the opportunity is. It does not tell you the links are yours. They still have to be earned, with a genuine pitch and something worth linking to. What it buys you is direction: you stop guessing, every prospect has a specific reason you are relevant, and your content targets are validated by the market instead of a hunch.

That is the whole idea behind aiming before you fire. The best off-page campaigns are half decided before the first email goes out, in the gap between where you are and where the sites ahead of you already sit. Close that gap deliberately, and the outreach gets easier because it finally has a target.

Reference: backlink intersect and keyword gap workflows as implemented in tools such as Ahrefs and Semrush.
