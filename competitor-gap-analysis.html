Competitor gap analysis: aim before you fire

By John Masajo · Published March 12, 2026 · 4 min read

A lot of link building starts with "let's get links." I start with a question: what's already working for the sites ranking above us, and can I earn the same links, then more? A gap analysis answers that. It's the difference between sending outreach everywhere and sending it where it has a real chance.

What a gap analysis is

It's a comparison. You put your backlinks and keywords next to your real competitors' and look for two things. First, sites that link to several of your competitors but not to you (called a link intersect). Second, keywords they rank for that you don't. Those two lists become your plan: one for outreach, one for content.

Why I do it first

Because it improves your chances before you send a single email. A site that already links to three of your competitors is much more likely to link to you than a random site that has never linked to anyone in your space. It has already shown it links to businesses like yours. Going after those sites first gets more replies and more placements, and wastes a lot less time. You're not asking someone to start linking. You're giving someone who already does a reason to add you.

How I run it (works in any tool)

The tools are different, but the steps are the same. This works in Ahrefs, Semrush, or any tool with a link intersect report.

1. Pick three to five real competitors. Not the biggest brands in your space, but the sites actually ranking for your main keywords. Their links are the ones that matter to you.

2. Run the link intersect. Pull the sites linking to two or more competitors but not to you. That list is the core of your prospecting, and it's already relevant because your market picked it.

3. Check each site before you count it. Relevance first, then authority against a sensible Domain Rating floor, then a quick manual check that it's a real editorial site and not a link farm. A high DR on a junk site is still junk.

4. Run the keyword gap. Find the keywords competitors rank for that you don't, and keep the ones you can realistically win. Those become your content targets: the pages worth building, or the pages your new links should point to.

5. Prioritize, then start outreach. Sort the list by relevance and how winnable each one is, not just by size, and start from the top. Now every pitch has a real reason behind it.

What it can and can't do

A gap analysis shows you where the opportunities are. It doesn't get you the links. You still have to earn them with a real pitch and something worth linking to. What it gives you is direction. You stop guessing, every prospect has a clear reason to care about you, and your content plan is based on what's already working in your market, not a hunch.

That's what I mean by aiming before you fire. A good off-page campaign is half planned before the first email goes out. Find the gap between you and the sites ahead of you, close it on purpose, and the outreach gets a lot easier.

I run this before every project. It's step one of how I work.

Reference: backlink intersect and keyword gap workflows as implemented in tools such as Ahrefs and Semrush.
