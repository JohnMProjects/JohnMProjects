Digital PR is now the #1 link-building tactic. Here's why.

By John Masajo · Published April 28, 2026 · 3 min read

For most of the last ten years, link building meant outreach at volume: guest posts, directory submissions, the odd broken-link email, sent in bulk and judged by how many landed. In 2026, the industry's own survey says the most effective tactic is something slower and harder: digital PR.

The number

The 2026 State of Link Building report asked SEOs which tactic actually works best. Digital PR came first. Nearly half of the people surveyed rated it the most effective, more than three times as many as picked guest posting. People running digital PR also reported strong returns, with average campaign ROI well into the triple digits. When the people doing the work rank something that far ahead, it's worth looking at why.

What digital PR is

It's getting coverage by giving a publication something worth writing about, like original data, a fresh expert view, or a timely story. The link comes naturally from that coverage. That's the main difference from other tactics. With guest posting, you write the article for them. With paid placements, you buy the link. With digital PR, an editor decides your input is worth including. The link is a result of being useful, not something you asked for.

Why it came out on top

Three reasons. First, it gets the kind of link Google trusts most: editorial, earned, and from a real publication with real readers. Second, the same coverage gets you brand mentions too, which now matter for showing up in AI answers. One placement helps with both. Third, it lasts. Editorial links hold up through algorithm updates and manual reviews, while bought or low-quality links tend to get ignored or disavowed later.

The catch

Digital PR is harder and slower than mass outreach. You need a real hook, like data you collected, an opinion worth quoting, or a story that fits the news. You also need relationships and patience, because a lot of pitches won't land. You can't fully automate it. That's exactly why it works. If it were easy, everyone would have the coverage, and it wouldn't mean much.

How I approach it

Start with the thing you're pitching, not the outreach. Build something worth covering, like original research, a data study, or a strong opinion backed by evidence. Then find the journalists and publications that already write about that topic, and pitch them the story, not a link request. Done right, the coverage brings links and mentions with it, and keeps bringing them long after the campaign is over.

The tactic that won is really just the basic idea behind good link building: be worth writing about. Digital PR gives that a process.
