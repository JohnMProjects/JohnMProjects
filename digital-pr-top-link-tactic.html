Digital PR is now the #1 link-building tactic. Here's why.

By John Masajo · Published April 28, 2026 · 3 min read

For most of the last decade, "link building" meant outreach at volume. Guest posts, directory submissions, the occasional broken-link email, sent in bulk and measured by how many landed. In 2026, the industry's own survey says the most effective tactic is something quieter and harder: digital PR.

The number

The 2026 State of Link Building report asked working SEOs which tactic actually delivers. Digital PR came out on top, rated the most effective by nearly half of respondents, more than three times the share that picked guest posting. Practitioners running it also reported strong returns, with average campaign ROI well into triple digits. When the people doing the work rank a tactic that far ahead, it is worth understanding why.

What digital PR actually is

It is earning coverage by giving a publication something genuinely worth writing about, original data, a fresh expert angle, a timely story, so that the link comes as a natural result of the coverage. That is the key difference from its cousins. In guest posting, you write the article for them. In paid placements, you buy the link. In digital PR, you earn a mention because an editor decided your input was worth including. The link is a byproduct of being useful, not the ask.

Why it climbed to number one

Three reasons, and they compound. First, it produces exactly the kind of link Google trusts most: editorial, earned, from a real publication with its own audience. Second, the same coverage produces brand mentions, not just links, which now matters for visibility in AI answers as much as the link itself. One earned placement feeds both scoreboards. Third, it is durable. Editorial links age well and survive algorithm updates and manual reviews, where bought or low-quality links get quietly devalued or disavowed later.

The honest catch

Digital PR is harder and slower than blasting outreach. It needs a real hook, data you gathered, a perspective worth quoting, a story with a timely angle, and it needs relationships and patience, because plenty of pitches will not land. You cannot fully automate your way through it. That difficulty is precisely why it works. If it were easy, everyone would have the coverage, and it would stop being a signal.

How I approach it

Start with the asset, not the outreach. Build something worth covering: a piece of original research, a data study, a strong contrarian take backed by evidence. Then find the specific journalists and publications who already write about that topic, and pitch them the angle, not a request for a link. Done right, the coverage arrives with links and mentions attached, and it keeps earning both long after the campaign ends.

The tactic that won is really the one that was always the point: be genuinely worth writing about. Digital PR just gives that instinct a process.

Source: State of Link Building 2026 (Aira and Editorial.link industry survey).
