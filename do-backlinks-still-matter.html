Do backlinks still matter in 2026? An honest answer.

By John Masajo · Published June 25, 2026 · 3 min read

Every year someone says backlinks are dead. In 2026 the new version is: "AI doesn't use links, so links don't matter anymore." I build links for a living, so keep that in mind. But the real answer isn't a simple yes or no.

For Google, links still matter a lot

Nothing changed here this year. Links are still one of the clearest ways to move your rankings. The 2026 core updates kept rewarding sites with authority and pushing down thin, copied sites, and a big part of authority still comes from who links to you. If you want to rank in normal search, and most businesses still get a lot of their traffic from it, you need backlinks.

For AI answers, it works differently

This is where the "links are dead" people are half right. Research on how AI tools pick their sources found they care more about clear, easy-to-quote statements and the overall reputation of a source than about how many backlinks it has. An AI tool isn't counting your links when it writes an answer. Your links still help, but indirectly: they make you more visible and more trusted, and that's what the AI picks up on.

So where does that leave us?

Backlinks aren't dead. They're one part of building authority. For Google, they're still a big part. For AI, they help indirectly, next to brand mentions and content that's easy to quote. The goal used to be "get links." Now it's "become a trusted source," and links are still one of the best ways to get there. They're just not the only way.

The mistake to avoid

Don't take "AI doesn't need links" as an excuse to cut corners, buy cheap links, or spam. If anything, the bar is higher now. The links worth getting are relevant, editorial, and from sites people already trust. Chasing link volume never worked well. Now it works even less.

So, do backlinks still matter? Yes. They're just not the whole answer anymore. Build them as part of becoming a real authority, not as a shortcut around it.
