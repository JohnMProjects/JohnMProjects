E-E-A-T in the AI era: the half everyone skips

By John Masajo · Published July 10, 2026 · 3 min read

E-E-A-T usually gets talked about like an on-page checklist. Add an author bio, list some credentials, cite your sources, done. That is a real part of it, but it is only half. The half that actually decides whether Google and, increasingly, AI trust you happens off your own site, where you have the least direct control.

A quick refresher

E-E-A-T stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust. The two E's are about the content and whoever made it: have they actually done the thing, and do they know the subject. The A and the T are about reputation: what the rest of the web says about you. That distinction is the whole point of this piece.

Why it matters more now

Research into how AI systems choose which sources to cite keeps landing on the same finding: they favor sources that are authoritative and well-regarded. That is essentially E-E-A-T by another name. So this is no longer just a Google ranking concept. It is close to what a language model is estimating when it decides whether to trust you enough to build an answer on you.

The on-site half

This part you control. Demonstrate genuine expertise and first-hand experience, make authorship clear, keep your facts accurate and current. Necessary, all of it. But here is the catch: you can do every bit of it and still not be trusted, because anyone can claim expertise on their own page. A claim you make about yourself is the weakest kind of evidence.

The off-site half, the one people skip

Authoritativeness and Trust are conferred, not declared. They are built by who links to you, who mentions you, who quotes you, and where. A specialist's site reads as authoritative partly because respected institutions link to it and real publications quote them. That is off-page work. You cannot self-certify authority. The web decides it, by talking about you or not. Which means the most important half of E-E-A-T is, quietly, an off-page job.

How to build the A and the T

Earn links and mentions from respected sources in your field. Get named as an expert through commentary, interviews, and roundups. Accumulate genuine reviews and citations. And stay consistent as an entity across the web, same name, same details, so both people and machines can connect the scattered signals into one trustworthy picture.

The honesty note

You cannot fake this. Bought authority, planted mentions, and fake reviews get discounted or penalized, and they defeat the entire purpose, which is to actually be trustworthy. Trust is earned slowly and lost quickly, which is exactly why it is worth so much when a search engine or a model finds it.

So treat E-E-A-T as two jobs, not one. Experience and Expertise you demonstrate on your pages. Authoritativeness and Trust you earn off them. Skip the second half and the first half never gets to count.

Authoritativeness and Trust are earned off-site, the work behind my case studies.
