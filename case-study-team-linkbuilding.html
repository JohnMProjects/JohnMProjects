Case Study · Team Campaign

Resource-page link building at agency scale

Published August 2025 · Engagement: September 2023 to August 2025 · UK marketing agency delivery team

This one is different from a single-client project. I was part of a Philippine team doing link building for a UK marketing agency. My job was to earn editorial links every month against a fixed target, for whichever of the agency's clients our team had at the time. What I can show here is the quality and consistency of the links I placed, not any one client's results. The section below explains why.

The mandate

The agency has a lot of clients and moves them between teams about every six months. So my team always had three clients at a time, and the clients changed more than once between September 2023 and August 2025. They were established brands, and the links went to their useful content, like data-based guides, statistics pages, and reference content, not to their product or service pages. My job was off-page: earn links to that content, hit 10 to 15 links a month, and do it cleanly.

How I worked

Most of my links came from resource pages. I found pages that list useful references on a client's topic, checked that the client's content was a good fit, and earned a spot on the page. Guest posts were a second option when a site accepted outside writers. Every link met the client's minimum Domain Rating, and every one was earned. No payments, no sponsored tags, no link swaps. A lot of "link building" out there is really just buying links. Sticking to earned links every month is harder, but those links last.

Why this study shows links, not client numbers

These are big clients. Their traffic and rankings depend on their brand, their budget, and a full team, so there's no honest way to separate what my links did from everything else. The work is also under NDA. So instead of using a client's numbers and making them look like mine, this case study shows what I can prove is mine: the links I placed and how strong the sites are. On a team, I take credit for my links. I don't take credit for the clients' results.

What I learned

Two things. First, being consistent is a skill on its own. Hitting a link target once can be luck. Hitting it every month, for three clients that keep changing, takes a system for finding good link opportunities fast. Second, sticking to earned links pays off over time. Resource page and guest post links hold up in audits, while bought links tend to get ignored or disavowed later. So staying earned-only was worth it, even when it was slower.
