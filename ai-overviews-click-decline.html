AI Overviews and the click decline: what the data really shows

By John Masajo · Published June 18, 2026 · 4 min read

The chart every SEO is worried about this year looks like this: rankings stay the same, but clicks go down. AI Overviews are the reason. The good news is there's now enough data to plan around it. Short version: the clicks didn't disappear. They moved.

The numbers everyone shares

AI Overviews now show up on about half of all searches, up from about a third a year ago. When one shows up, the top organic result loses close to a fifth of its clicks. And in Google's AI Mode, most sessions end without a click to any website. On their own, those numbers look bad.

The part people leave out

Pages that get cited inside an AI Overview get more clicks, about a third more than the same page would get from a normal listing. So the clicks went to whoever the AI quoted. Ranking on page one but not being in the AI answer is starting to feel like being on page two.

What changed

The goal used to be "rank number one and get the click." Now it's also "be the source the AI answer is built from." That comes down to three things: writing clear statements an AI can pull out and quote, having real authority (AI tools prefer trusted, well-known sources), and getting mentioned and linked on the third-party sites AI relies on. That last one is off-page work, which is why I think off-page skills became more useful this year, not less.

How to measure it now

Don't judge results on clicks alone anymore. I'd watch four things instead. First, whether your brand shows up when you ask AI tools a question in your space. Second, your impressions inside AI Overviews, which Google Search Console now reports separately. Third, branded searches and direct traffic, from people who saw you in an answer and came back later. Fourth, assisted conversions, which catch visits that last-click reports miss.

If traffic drops while impressions and citations go up, that's not failure. That's just how the funnel looks now.

One thing to accept

Some searches will never send a click again, because the AI answer already covers everything. For those, the win is that people saw your brand. Accept it, focus on the searches where a click is still possible, and try to be the cited source everywhere else.

AI Overviews didn't kill the click. They moved it to the sources that get cited. Track the new numbers, aim to be the source and not just the ranking, and the chart gets a lot less scary.

Being the cited source comes down to authority and trust. More on that in E-E-A-T in the AI era.
