UK CBD Brand: Two-Site Ranking Defense

Published November 2025 · Engagement: March to November 2025

A link building project for a CBD brand with two sister sites. This wasn't about growing from zero. The main UK site already ranked in the top 3 for its most competitive keywords, so my job was to keep it there and push it further, while a brand-new Italian site got its first rankings. I handled the link building and wrote the content briefs.

The challenge

The client had two sister sites in the CBD space, and they were complete opposites. The UK site was established and already ranked in the top 3 for the most competitive keywords in its category. A small group of those keywords brought in about three-quarters of its traffic. So those rankings were its biggest asset and its biggest risk: losing them would cost far more than any new keyword could bring in. Its Domain Rating was already around 38, where every extra point gets much harder to earn. Its weaker product areas, vape and CBD oil, were stuck in the middle of the results, and on blog pages instead of product pages. The Italian site was the opposite: a brand-new .it domain at around DR 14, with almost no links and almost no traffic. So one site needed protecting and the other needed starting, at the same time.

I worked on this alone. A UK-based SEO consultant assigned and checked my work, and handled the client.

The plan

Two things at once. On the UK site, protect the top-3 rankings first, then push the keywords that were close. Use relevant editorial and niche links that met the client's DR 20 minimum, and write content briefs so the authority went to the product pages, not the blog. On the Italian site, build its first backlinks to the same DR 20 standard, knowing the numbers would be small and that rankings and referring domains would tell the story better than early traffic.

What I did

I got links for both sites through relevant editorial and niche placements, picked directory and citation listings, and HARO-style expert quotes and roundups. I focused on relevance first and checked every site in Ahrefs against the DR 20 minimum. Cannabis, CBD, and vape community sites and real lifestyle blogs came before generic high-volume sites. For every page that needed to hold or gain a ranking, I also wrote a content brief (topic, meta title and description, keywords, and internal links), so the authority landed on the product pages and not random blog posts.

To keep the numbers honest, I only counted links first seen during the months I worked on the project. I removed every link between the two sister sites, counted each referring domain once across both, and set aside search engine and auto-generated links instead of counting them as outreach. Every link I report met the DR 20 minimum.

A small group of keywords brought in about three-quarters of the traffic, so keeping those rankings mattered more than adding new keywords. Domain Rating only went up a little, since every point above DR 30 is much harder to earn.

What didn't work: the vape pen and CBD oil keywords didn't reach page one. They're still in the middle of the results on blog pages instead of product pages. Fixing that is the next priority for links and content. It's not solved.

Site 2: New Italian-market sister

A new .it site with almost no traffic at the start. The goal was its first rankings and first backlinks.

Traffic rose from 9 to a peak of 144, then settled to 38 per month.

Traffic 9 to 38/mo (August peak 144) · DR ~14 · tracked keywords 73 to 133 · keywords in the top 10 went from 3 to 12 · first top-3 rankings (0 to 3). I'm showing the full traffic line, not just the peak: it went up to 144 in August, then dropped back to 38 by November. The links here are few but real: an Italian fashion and lifestyle blog with a relevant local anchor, a placement on an expert-quote platform, and mentions on cannabis-friendly lifestyle sites. For a site this new, quality over volume was the only honest option. Its authority stayed low, so this is a story about first rankings, not about authority.

What I learned

Three things. First, protecting rankings is its own job: holding the few keywords that bring most of the traffic is worth more than adding lots of new keywords, and it takes cleaner, more relevant links. Second, traffic estimates for a new site in a small market jump around a lot, so I focused on rankings and referring domains instead of a traffic line that went from 9 to 144 and back. Third, pairing links with content briefs made sure the authority went to the product pages that make sales, not scattered blog posts.
