Brand mentions vs backlinks: the shift AI forced on link building

By John Masajo · Published April 22, 2026 · 3 min read

For years, off-page SEO had one scoreboard: backlinks. The more authoritative and relevant the site linking to you, the better you did. That is still true for Google rankings. But AI search added a second scoreboard in the last year, and it does not count links the way we are used to.

The number that reframed the job

Ahrefs ran the study that made this concrete. Across 75,000 brands, they found that branded web mentions correlate far more strongly with visibility in AI answers than backlinks do, roughly 0.66 for mentions against 0.22 for links. In plain terms, being talked about tracked about three times more closely with showing up in AI results than being linked to.

The reason is mechanical, not magic. Large language models are trained on and retrieve web text where your brand appears in context. A model can learn that your company is a strong answer to a question purely from how often, and how well, your name shows up on sources it trusts. A clickable hyperlink is not required for that association to form.

What this changes for off-page work

It does not mean links are dead. It means the deliverable got wider. There are now two goals sitting side by side. The first is the old one: earn authoritative, relevant links, still the most direct lever you have on Google rankings. The second is newer: build a steady footprint of brand mentions across the sources AI leans on, linked or not.

The good news for anyone who already does real off-page work is that the same skill produces both. Earning genuine coverage, an expert quote in an industry publication, an inclusion in a credible roundup, a mention on a respected community site, tends to bring a link and a mention together. You are not learning a new trade. You are being measured on a bigger surface of it.

The honest caveat

Mentions you manufacture yourself do not count, and they carry risk. Google's 2026 guidance on AI features warns specifically against chasing inauthentic brand mentions seeded across forums and blogs, and its spam systems are built to catch that kind of manipulation. The mentions that move AI visibility are the ones you earn on sources that already have trust. Faking the signal is the fast way to lose it.

How I would adjust a program

Three practical shifts. Track brand mentions next to referring domains, so the report reflects both scoreboards. Prioritize placements on the specific sources that AI tools already cite in your niche, rather than chasing raw domain authority alone. And add one new check to your reporting: does the brand actually appear when you ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Mode a relevant question. That is the outcome the mentions are meant to produce.

The metric expanded from "who links to you" to "who talks about you, and where." For a link builder, that is not a threat. It is a larger, more interesting surface to work, and it rewards the one thing that was always hard to fake: being genuinely worth mentioning.

Sources: Ahrefs, 2026 study of 75,000 brands on mentions and AI visibility; Google Search Central, 2026 guide to optimizing for generative AI features.
