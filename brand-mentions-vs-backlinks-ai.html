Brand mentions vs backlinks: the shift AI forced on link building

By John Masajo · Published April 22, 2026 · 3 min read

For years, off-page SEO had one main score: backlinks. The more trusted and relevant the site linking to you, the better. That's still true for Google rankings. But in the last year, AI search added a second score, and it doesn't count links the way we're used to.

The number that changed things

Ahrefs studied 75,000 brands. They found that brand mentions on the web line up much more closely with showing up in AI answers than backlinks do: about 0.66 for mentions versus 0.22 for links. Put simply, being talked about was about three times more connected to showing up in AI results than being linked to.

The reason makes sense. AI tools learn from and read web pages where your brand is mentioned. An AI can learn that your company is a good answer just from how often, and how well, your name shows up on sites it trusts. It doesn't need a clickable link for that.

What this means for off-page work

Links aren't dead. The job just got bigger. There are now two goals. The first is the same as before: earn trusted, relevant links, which are still the most direct way to move Google rankings. The second is newer: get your brand mentioned regularly on the sites AI relies on, with or without a link.

The good part is that the same work usually gets you both. When you earn real coverage, like an expert quote in an industry publication, a spot in a good roundup, or a mention on a respected community site, you often get a link and a mention at the same time. It's not a new job. It's the same job, measured on more things.

Be careful with this

Mentions you make up yourself don't count, and they can hurt you. Google's 2026 guidance on AI features warns against fake brand mentions planted on forums and blogs, and its spam systems are built to catch that. The mentions that help are the ones you earn on sites that are already trusted. Faking it is the fastest way to lose it.

What I'd change in a link building plan

Three things. Track brand mentions next to referring domains, so the report shows both. Go after the sites AI tools already cite in your niche, not just the ones with the highest domain authority. And add one simple check to your reports: does the brand show up when you ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Mode a relevant question? That's the result the mentions are supposed to get you.

The question used to be "who links to you." Now it's also "who talks about you, and where." For a link builder, that's not bad news. It's just more ground to cover, and it still rewards the same thing: being worth mentioning.

Earning both links and mentions is the work behind my case studies.

Sources: Ahrefs, 2026 study of 75,000 brands on mentions and AI visibility; Google Search Central, 2026 guide to optimizing for generative AI features.
