The case

The client was a UK travel brand running three sister sites: an established flagship plus two newer properties that had barely any backlink profile to speak of. They needed search authority built across all three, fast enough to matter but clean enough to last. My brief was off-page, brand-wide, with room to shape the content that the links would point to.

What I did

I owned link building across all three sites, earning referring domains through four channels: curated directory and citation placements, guest-post outreach, and HARO-style expert-insight and roundup contributions where a journalist or blogger was collecting quotes. Every placement was vetted to a minimum Domain Rating of 15 in Ahrefs before I pursued it.

I also supported the on-page side. For the content that needed to rank, I wrote briefs for the writer: the target topic and working title, the meta title and description, the keywords to target, and the internal linking structure for each piece. That kept new pages aligned with the link strategy, so the authority I was building actually landed on pages positioned to use it.

How I did it

The work ran on a simple discipline. Prospect against a DR 15 floor, so no low-value links diluted the profile. Spread acquisition across directories, editorial guest posts, and expert roundups, so the profile looked earned rather than bulk-built. And keep the content briefs flowing to the writer so on-page and off-page moved together instead of in isolation.

Because the three sites were sister properties, I also stripped out every link they made to each other before reporting results. A link from a domain the same company owns is not earned authority, and I did not want the numbers inflated by cross-linking. I deduplicated referring domains across the three sites as well, so a directory that listed all three counts once, not three times.

Property 1: Established flagship

The oldest domain, already carrying authority when the engagement began.

Estimated organic traffic · monthly

Traffic +77% (1,053 to 1,868/mo) · DR ~22 to ~26 · 45 referring domains · 68 live backlinks. Already an established domain, so gains showed up more in traffic than in raw authority score. Total keyword count held steady while priority target keywords were driven into the top 1 to 3, concentrating the gains in positions that convert.

Property 2: Biggest mover

A newer property where the off-page build translated into the strongest growth.

Estimated organic traffic · monthly

Traffic +255% (315 to 1,119/mo) · DR 11 to 16 · 44 referring domains · 173 live backlinks. The strongest result of the three: a higher-quality earned link profile paired with real keyword growth. Tracked keywords rose 31% and top-3 rankings doubled.

Property 3: Newest site, authority focus

The smallest, newest property, where the work was foundational authority-building.

DR 9 to 16 · 8 referring domains · 5 live backlinks. The largest Domain Rating lift of the three, nearly doubling authority on a site starting from a low base. Organic traffic on this smaller property stayed volatile and never set a clear trend, including a one-off July spike that I do not treat as a result. The goal here was the foundational backlink profile, and the DR growth reflects that.

What I learned

Three lessons came out of this engagement. First, authority does not translate evenly: the same off-page playbook produced a 255% traffic lift on one property and flat, noisy traffic on another, because site age, size, and content readiness matter as much as the links. Second, small sites produce unreliable traffic estimates, so I learned to lead with Domain Rating and referring domains on those, not tool-estimated traffic that can swing wildly month to month. Third, pairing the link work with content briefs mattered: the property where new pages were built around the target keywords and internal linking was also the property that moved most.
